# Пресс-пакет: Bali & Phuket Delivery Benchmark 2026

Цель — не публикация про компанию, а публикация про данные. Издание берёт материал
тогда, когда у него есть цифра, которой нет больше нигде, и понятный локальный сюжет.
У нас это есть: таких данных по ЮВА не публикует никто, включая сами площадки.

Побочный, но главный для нас эффект: два-три независимых упоминания в местных изданиях
закрывают претензию «всё self-reported» и открывают Wikidata.

Правило: рассылаем **по одному письму на издание, с индивидуальным первым абзацем**.
Одинаковая рассылка на десять адресов читается как спам и убивает шанс со всеми сразу.

## 1. Пресс-релиз (EN, основной текст)

**Заголовок:**
`Bali restaurants lose about a quarter of their delivery revenue — and 95% of it to menu items nobody switched back on`

**Подзаголовок:**
`First aggregated benchmark of GrabFood and GoFood performance in Bali and Phuket: 96 restaurants, 270,568 orders`

```
Delivery Booster, a Bali-based agency that manages GrabFood and GoFood accounts for
restaurants across Indonesia and Thailand, has published the first aggregated benchmark of
restaurant delivery performance in Bali and Phuket. The report covers 96 restaurants the
agency manages — 84 in Bali, 12 in Phuket — and 270,568 orders placed between January and
August 2026. All figures are medians, and all restaurant-level data is anonymised.

The headline finding contradicts what most owners assume. Restaurants do not lose delivery
revenue while they are closed. Across the Bali fleet roughly 25% of potential revenue is
lost, and 95% of that loss comes from menu items switched off and never switched back on.
Restaurant downtime accounts for 3% of the loss and cancellations for 2%.

The report contains dishes that sat out of stock for more than 2,000 hours — 84 consecutive
days. Individual venues had between 40 and 70 items switched off simultaneously while
continuing to pay for advertising that sent customers to that menu.

The second finding concerns advertising. Delivery ads stop paying back at around 6% of
revenue: below that line the median return on ad spend is 12.1x, above it 8.6x. 42% of the
restaurants in the sample are already past that threshold.

Reference medians for Bali: Rp 250,000 average check, 10.4x return on ad spend, 5.6% of
revenue spent on advertising, a 0.35% cancellation rate and one negative review per 138
orders. Reviews are strongly bimodal — 51% five-star and 28% one-star, with only 3%
four-star — which means a restaurant's rating is moved by removing one-star reviews rather
than by improving averages.

Phuket behaves like a less saturated market: an average check of USD 22.5 against USD 15.2
in Bali, a 22.8x return on ad spend against 10.4x, and 2.9% of revenue spent on advertising
against 5.5%.

"Everyone assumes the money leaks while the restaurant is closed," says Aleksei Mazur,
founder of Delivery Booster and co-founder of the Bali restaurant groups IKA Sushi and
FoodLab. "It leaks while the restaurant is open with half the menu switched off. Nobody is
auditing a 120-item menu across two platforms every morning, so items stay off for weeks."

Delivery Booster states one caveat directly in the report: the sample consists of
restaurants under active agency management and is not a random sample of the market.
Losses across the wider market are likely to be higher than the 25% measured here.

The full report, including a nine-area breakdown of Bali and the methodology, is published
free and without registration at booster.delivery/benchmark. The agency says it will update
the benchmark quarterly.

About Delivery Booster: founded in 2023 and headquartered in Bali (PT Delivery Booster
Group), the agency manages GrabFood and GoFood accounts on behalf of restaurants —
110+ restaurants under management today, 200+ served since 2023 — across Indonesia and
Thailand.

Media contact: Aleksei Mazur, founder — booster.delivery
```

## 2. Письма в издания

Формат: короткое письмо, цифра в первой строке, ссылка, предложение дать данные в удобном

### 2.1 The Bali Sun — `admin@thebalisun.com` (подтверждён)

**Subject:** `Data: Bali restaurants lose ~25% of delivery revenue — 96 restaurants, 270,568 orders`

```
Hello,

We manage GrabFood and GoFood accounts for 96 restaurants in Bali and Phuket, and we have
just published the first aggregated benchmark of how restaurant delivery actually performs
here: 270,568 orders, January to August 2026, anonymised and reported as medians.

The finding that surprised us most is a Bali story: restaurants lose roughly 25% of their
delivery revenue, and 95% of that loss is menu items switched off and never switched back
on — not closures (3%) and not cancellations (2%). We found dishes that sat out of stock
for 84 consecutive days, and venues with 40 to 70 items off at once while still paying for
ads pointing at that menu.

Other Bali medians, if useful: Rp 250,000 average check, 10.4x return on ad spend, 0.35%
cancellations, one negative review per 138 orders.

Nobody in Southeast Asia publishes this data, the platforms included. It is free and open
at booster.delivery/benchmark, and we are happy to provide the underlying medians, area
breakdowns or charts in whatever format suits you, and to answer questions on the method.

We state the bias in the report itself: this is a sample of agency-managed restaurants, so
the real market numbers are probably worse.

Aleksei Mazur
Founder, Delivery Booster (PT Delivery Booster Group), Bali
booster.delivery
```

### 2.3 The Phuket News / The Thaiger — адреса уточнить

**Subject:** `Data: Phuket delivery outperforms Bali 2:1 on ad returns — 96 restaurants, 270,568 orders`

Первый абзац — под Пхукет:

```
We manage GrabFood accounts for restaurants in Phuket and GrabFood/GoFood in Bali, and have
just published aggregated performance data for both: 270,568 orders, January to August 2026.

The Phuket angle: this is a far less saturated delivery market than Bali. Average check
USD 22.5 against USD 15.2, return on ad spend 22.8x against 10.4x, and advertising takes
2.9% of revenue here against 5.5% in Bali. Revenue leakage to out-of-stock items is 6% in
Phuket against 25% in Bali.
```

### 2.4 Katadata / Databoks и Tech in Asia — адреса уточнить

Эти живут на данных, поэтому письмо другое: предлагаем не новость, а датасет.

**Subject:** `Original dataset: 96 restaurants, 270,568 GrabFood/GoFood orders, Indonesia + Thailand`

```
Databoks regularly publishes food-delivery market data for Indonesia, so this may be useful:
we have aggregated operating data from 96 restaurants we manage on GrabFood and GoFood —
84 in Bali, 12 in Phuket, 270,568 orders, January to August 2026 — and published it openly
as medians.

It covers what platform-level market reports do not: median average check, return on ad
spend by ad-spend band, the point where ads stop paying back (around 6% of revenue),
cancellation rates, review distribution, and where revenue is lost inside the merchant
account. Also a Bali/Phuket comparison and a breakdown across nine areas of Bali.

We are happy to share the underlying medians in a spreadsheet, to run additional cuts on
request, and to be credited or not, as you prefer. The report is free and open:
booster.delivery/benchmark

Method and bias are stated in the report: agency-managed sample, medians rather than
averages, anonymised at restaurant level.
```
