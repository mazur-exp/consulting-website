**The money Phuket restaurants lose on Grab is not the commission**

*By Aleksei Mazur, founder of Delivery Booster, which manages GrabFood accounts for restaurants in Phuket and Bali*

Every restaurant owner in Phuket who sells through Grab knows one number by heart: 30 per cent. That is the commission, it is deducted at payout, and it is the line everyone complains about. After three years of reading merchant dashboards — more than 200 restaurants have passed through our management since 2023, over 110 are with us today, mostly in Indonesia, a dozen here in Phuket, and this year's published data alone covers 270,000 orders — I am fairly sure the commission is the wrong thing to worry about. It is a known cost. The money that actually disappears is the money nobody is looking at.

A caveat first: twelve Phuket outlets is a small sample for Phuket-specific numbers, and I will not pretend otherwise. But the patterns below are not Phuket patterns — they are the same ones we have seen on two hundred restaurants across two countries, and Phuket simply repeats them at its own scale. What follows is what I would tell a friend opening a restaurant in Rawai or Bang Tao.

**The leak is the menu, not the fee**

In our Bali fleet, roughly a quarter of delivery revenue is lost before it is earned, and 95 per cent of that loss comes from one thing: menu items switched off "for now" and never switched back on. The kitchen runs out of a sauce at eleven in the morning, someone taps the item off in the app, and it stays off. We found dishes that had been out of stock for more than 2,000 hours — eighty-four days — while the restaurant was paying for ads pointing customers at that very menu.

Phuket is better, at about six per cent of revenue, but the structure is identical: 93 per cent of what is lost here is switched-off items. Closures and cancellations, the things owners tend to blame, are a rounding error. Our Phuket accounts had four cancelled orders in 11,403 over twelve months.

So the first piece of advice is boring and it is the most valuable one: open the report of switched-off items every morning, and put someone's name on it. Not "the kitchen". A name.

**Phuket's season makes every mistake twice as expensive**

The island roughly doubles between June and January. Our tourist tracker puts the average number of visitors on Phuket at about 197,000 a day in June and 349,000 in January. That swing changes what a mistake costs. A dish switched off in a quiet week in June loses you a few orders; the same dish switched off in January loses you a few orders every hour, and the customers who see "unavailable" are the ones who will not come back this trip.

Three things follow from that. Build your rating before November, not during the season, because one one-star review costs about twenty five-stars to cancel out, and in January the review flow is too heavy to fix anything. Put English and Russian names on every dish with a photo: Russians are the largest group on the island, about 162,000 in January, and a tourist does not open a menu they cannot read. And keep your opening hours in the app honest — an order cancelled because you were "open" but not there hurts your place in the feed more than a closed sign does.

**Advertising works here — which is exactly why you should spend less on it**

This is the counterintuitive one. Across our Phuket outlets, Grab advertising pays back around 23 times what it costs, against about 10 times in Bali. Owners hear that and reach for the budget. Do not. That kind of return means the market is not saturated and impressions are cheap; it does not mean more money buys proportionally more orders. In our data the payback falls off a cliff past roughly six per cent of revenue: below that line the median return is about 12 times, above it under nine. Our Phuket outlets spend under three per cent of revenue on ads, and I would keep it there through the season.

The same logic applies to the offers the platforms make from time to time: a lower commission in exchange for exclusivity, meaning you drop LINE MAN or you drop Grab. With two apps covering nearly the whole market between them, giving up one of them to save a few points of commission has never paid off in any case I have seen.

**What the 30 per cent buys, and what it does not**

The commission buys you the customer base of the app, the riders, the payments and the support. It does not buy you a place in the feed. That is decided by your rating, how fast you accept orders, how complete your menu is and how often you cancel — in other words, by how the account is run day to day. That is the part that is entirely in the owner's hands, and it is the part most owners hand to whoever is standing nearest the tablet.

If you take one thing from two hundred restaurants and three years of dashboards, take this: stop arguing with the commission and start reading the stock-out report. The first is a cost of doing business. The second is money you already earned and then gave away.

*Aleksei Mazur is the founder of Delivery Booster, a delivery management agency that has run GrabFood and GoFood accounts for more than 200 restaurants in Indonesia and Thailand since 2023, with over 110 under management today. The company publishes its aggregated data openly at booster.delivery/benchmark.*
